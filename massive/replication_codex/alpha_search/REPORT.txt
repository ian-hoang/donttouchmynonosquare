ALPHA SEARCH: EXECUTION, SECTOR CONTROLS AND A NEW COUNTERPARTY IDEA

No new independently validated alpha was found. The strongest existing candidate
remains the acquisition short-call overlay, conditional on its original eligibility
rules. It survives quoted bid/ask costs. A broader version fails in reused 2026,
and the sector comparison remains too uncertain to establish a reliable new edge.
This report records the failures as well as the favorable numbers.

1. THE ORIGINAL ELIGIBLE TRADES SURVIVE STRICTER EXECUTION

Keep the frozen contracts and 45 eligible event trades. Simulate selling at the
entry bid and buying at the exit ask, five minutes before the regular close,
requiring quotes no more than five minutes old and displayed size on both sides.
Charge $0.65 per contract per side. All 45 trades have usable quotes at both ends.

                                      2024-25             Reused Jan-Aug 2026
Eligible event trades                 30                  15
Mean option-overlay net               +0.5706%            +0.6394%
Win rate                              66.67%              66.67%
Unannualized per-trade mean/SD         0.2961              0.3156

Returns are per dollar of entry parity-stock reference notional, excluding the
underlying shares' profit or loss. These are simulated displayed-quote fills,
not observed executions or returns on margin. Assignment, dividends, funding,
latency and market impact remain unmodeled.

Across the original 20 quiet-peer draws, diagnostic event-minus-peer short-call
gaps remain positive in every seed. But actually BUYING those peer calls incurs
another spread: the short-event/long-peer basket averages +0.6006% and +0.3266%
per event-stock reference notional; only 16/20 2026 seeds are positive. Divide
by two for combined gross stock-reference notional. Those original peer draws
use future disclosures in the quiet filter, so they are retrospective comparisons,
not a prospectively selectable trading portfolio.

2. A FIXED BROADER TEST WITH PROSPECTIVELY SELECTABLE SECTOR CONTROLS

Before fetching returns, PROSPECTIVE_SPEC.txt froze a separate test. It includes
all 59 kept events, retains the original contract selection, and replaces daily
trade-mark availability with fresh option quotes. It samples up to three sector
peers using seed 1729 and only filings known by the event filing date. Future
filings cannot change peer eligibility. Peers missing at entry are not admitted;
an admitted peer missing at exit leaves the basket unpriced rather than disappearing.

The issuer-cluster bootstrap interval for the broad 2026 overlay is -2.65% to
+1.11%; the sector-gap interval is -2.22 to +1.99 percentage points. Month-block
and filing-date intervals are also saved. These descriptive intervals are not
adjusted for the thousands of strategy variants previously searched. There are
only 13 issuer clusters for the broader 2026 comparison, so uncertainty is high.

WHAT CHANGED THE 2026 SIGN?

Three events previously failed the original pre-event ATM daily-mark requirement
but had fresh quotes:
  CHTR 2026-01-06: +2.1753%
  ABBV 2026-06-22: -5.5864%
  CHTR 2026-07-23: -17.0333%

The large losses survive independent checks of quote timestamps, bid/ask sides,
fees, parity denominators and contract metadata before entry and at exit. No
adjusted-deliverable anomaly is apparent. The three events' contracts remain
standard 100-share contracts in the supplied metadata.

Crucial distinction: the original pre-event mark rule is observable BEFORE entry.
Adding these events changes the strategy's eligibility. Their losses do not prove
lookahead in that rule and do not invalidate the successful frozen replication.
They do show that removing the eligibility rule is not an innocuous extension.

On exactly the original eligible cohort, fresh parity denominators give +0.5725%
and +0.6489%, close to the strict-execution results above. This post-result
decomposition diagnoses cohort changes; it is not a newly selected strategy.
For that original cohort with the newly fixed same-sector controls, the 2026
comparison has 14 priced baskets, an average gap of +1.0403 pp and an issuer-
cluster interval of -0.5533 to +2.5923 pp. The actual pair averages +0.2636% per
event reference notional, with an interval of -1.3364% to +1.8116%. It is not
strong enough to label a verified tradable relative-value edge.

3. NEW HYPOTHESIS: AN 8-K HURTS A NAMED ECONOMIC COUNTERPARTY

The separate frozen screen looked for operational disruption or actual purchase
cancellation disclosed by any issuer that harms a named company in the tradable
universe. It screened 2,052 vendor disclosure rows from 1,945 unique 2024-25
filings across five categories and audited 183 third-party name matches.
Zero qualifying events were source-validated; no prices were fetched and the
minimum event rule was not weakened. The nearest cases were a terminated Dell/
VMware distribution framework without the required evidence of cancelled committed
purchases, and Krispy Kreme/McDonald's where the actual exhibit describes the
business as non-material to McDonald's. Most other matches were financing or
administrative relationships. The limited excerpt/alias screen is not proof that
no such events exist in the full filing universe.

4. WHAT THIS SUPPORTS

The narrow acquisition rule is a research candidate with reproducible positive
overlay P&L under fresh quoted costs. We have not established new independent
alpha. The broader event cohort and an executable peer hedge are fragile, and
the new counterparty hypothesis has insufficient validated observations.
The 2026 window has already been used; this work creates no new holdout evidence.
There was no reversal of losing rules, best-seed selection or parameter sweep.
Original frozen replication files remain unchanged.
