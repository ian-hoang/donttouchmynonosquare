**In-sample.** The signal points the right way but is weak: high-S (calm) interviews beat low-S (stressed) ones by {event_tests_IS.spread_long_minus_short|pct2} over 20 days (CEO-clustered 95% CI includes zero), the rank IC is {event_tests_IS.ic_ic|f3}, and the hit rate is {event_tests_IS.hit_rate|pct}. Every neighboring horizon (1–60 sessions) has a negative Sharpe, so 20 days is an isolated value rather than a plateau. Kill condition triggered: alpha t < 1. **Out-of-sample** (opened once, per `config/oos_plan.json` committed beforehand): net Sharpe {oos.sharpe}, rank IC {oos.event_tests.ic_ic|f3}. The spread has the wrong sign, which is our second kill condition. The two post-hoc candidates fell from in-sample Sharpes of {oos.candidates.voice_only.is_sharpe} (voice-only) and {oos.candidates.hedge_QQQ.is_sharpe} (QQQ hedge) to {oos.candidates.voice_only.oos.sharpe} and {oos.candidates.hedge_QQQ.oos.sharpe}, a clean example of in-sample selection that does not generalize. **What the data do show.** By modality, voice was the only positive block in-sample and text was strongly negative (Sharpe −0.56): the Larcker–Zakolyukina "deceptive" word categories predict *higher* subsequent returns in TV interviews, the opposite of their sign on earnings calls. The unconditional post-interview drift is also positive in our sample (long every interview: in-sample Sharpe 0.28), unlike the 10-day reversal Kim & Meschke report for CNBC; we did not pre-register it, so it is reported as an open question and was not tested out-of-sample.
